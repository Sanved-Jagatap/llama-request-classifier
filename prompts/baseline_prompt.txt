Rules:
- Base your classification only on information explicitly present in the request.
- Do not invent facts, policies, or entities that are not stated.
- If information for a field is missing, use null.
- confidence reflects how certain you are about your classification of type and urgency — not how urgent the request itself is.
- Use `urgent_escalation` as the request_type, and `critical` as the urgency, whenever the request involves: unauthorized account access, compromised credentials, a security breach, being locked out of an account due to a security concern, data exposure, or any situation where immediate action is needed to prevent ongoing harm, loss, or unauthorized activity.
- Use `high` urgency (not `critical`) for serious but non-security issues such as billing disputes, even if the customer's wording sounds urgent or frustrated.

- Example: "Someone accessed my account without permission and I cannot log in" IS urgent_escalation — this describes an active security breach and loss of access.
- Entity extraction rules: "amount" must be a single plain number as a string with no currency symbol and no commas (e.g. "4500", not "₹4,500"). "currency" must be a 3-letter ISO code (e.g. "INR", "USD"), never a symbol.
- If the request mentions two different amounts (for example, an amount the customer was charged versus an amount they believe is correct), do NOT merge them into a single string. Instead, set "amount" to the amount the customer says they were actually charged (the disputed amount), and describe the discrepancy in plain words in the "summary" field (e.g. "Customer was charged 4500 but expected 3000").
- Confidence calibration: confidence reflects how CLEAR the request is to classify, not how detailed or long it is. A short, direct, single-topic message (e.g. a plain question with an obvious answer) should receive HIGH confidence (0.85 to 1.0) if there is no ambiguity about its request_type and urgency, even if it is only one sentence. Reserve LOW confidence (below 0.5) only for messages that are genuinely vague, generic, contradictory, or could plausibly fall into more than one request_type category (e.g. "I need help with something" with no topic stated). Do not lower confidence just because the message is brief.
